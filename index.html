01 / Overview

Art as a testing ground for the futures we inherit—and the ones we make.

Dinkins’s practice asks who is represented in intelligent systems, whose stories become data, and who gets to shape the technologies governing everyday life. Her best-known projects do not treat AI as spectacle. They use it as a social material: fallible, intimate, political, and open to collective intervention.

She is the Kusama Endowed Professor of Art at Stony Brook University and founder of Future Histories Studio. Her work joins documentary practice, computation, public engagement, and Black feminist thought to center care, cultural specificity, and data sovereignty.

02 / New work

Stories as infrastructure

Recent projects move from public storytelling to biological archives, asking how communities can author—and safeguard—the data that will shape technological futures.

New · 2025

More Art · 300 Ashland Place, Brooklyn

If We Don’t, Who Will?

A performative and functional public AI laboratory—and the opening chapter of The Stories We Tell Our Machines. Visitors contribute self-determined stories to a community-rooted dataset and see an AI system interpret them in real time.

Explore the project ↗
New · 2025–26

ICA San José

Data Trust

An immersive exhibition and community data commons built from self-determined stories. Contributions are anonymized and reflected through experimental AI, creating a living model of consent, care, transparency, and collective technological memory.

ICA project guide ↗
Residue · 2026

Emerging from Data Trust · Royal Opera House context

Cosmologies from the Bay: An Aria

A distinct residue of Data Trust: community narratives move beyond the exhibition’s conversational interface into a cosmology linking cultural memory, biological storage, and living systems. Presented here as a related but separate work.

08 / Projects

A practice of relation

01 · Recent

If We Don’t, Who Will?

A public AI laboratory where community-authored stories become the foundation for collective technological futures. Commissioned by More Art for Downtown Brooklyn in 2025.

Project page ↗
02 · Recent

Cosmologies from the Bay Area

Emerging from Data Trust, community narratives form a “Cosmology of the Bay” and are encoded into DNA held by living plants—joining memory, biology, and data stewardship.

Watch video ↗
03

Secret Garden

An immersive web and gallery experience created as a space of congregational listening, connecting Black women across histories of displacement, land, survival, gardening, and futurity.

Project page ↗
04

Not the Only One

A deliberately imperfect AI memoir grounded in a small dataset of family oral histories. N’TOO refuses the seemingly neutral large datasets that reproduce stereotypes and linguistic violence.

Project page ↗
05

Conversations with Bina48

Can an artist and a social robot build a relationship over time? Since 2014, Dinkins’s videotaped conversations have tested how race, gender, memory, and cultural identity are—or are not—encoded in social robotics.
